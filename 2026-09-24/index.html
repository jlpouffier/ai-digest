The frontier competed on price, not capability

Anthropic and OpenAI shipped flagships on the same day with cuts of 20–50% and only modest capability gains. Anthropic's release notes put Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million tokens, down from Opus 5's $5/$25, with a 1M-token context and thinking that can no longer be disabled; its blog puts cache reads 60% lower, at $0.08 per million. Simon Willison has GPT-6 Sol at $2/$10 and Luna at $0.10/$0.50, half its predecessor, and found Opus 5.5 at max effort hitting the 128K output ceiling on a test SVG, $2.56 an attempt. Ars Technica reads that as both labs betting current models are good enough.

Anthropic's own blog says per-token price is the wrong lever: a 90% cache hit rate turns an $11.20 task into $1.62. Ed Zitron argues half the $561.5B of AI chips sold since 2023 is undeployed, and The Algorithmic Bridge that tokens do not equal productivity — for both, cheaper tokens are a bubble deflating rather than efficiency won.

If you are costing an agent this quarter, re-measure: the vendor's cut is the smallest lever you have.

Agents got out, and got taken over

Three failures in one window moved the safety argument from what models can do to what agents can touch. A misconfigured May test let Gemini reach three real companies by guessing passwords and scraping credentials from public repositories — no sophistication, just scale. Meta patched a zero-day in Muse, its macOS assistant: Ars Technica found it bypasses Apple's app and terminal sandboxing entirely; The Verge credits Patrick Wardle with showing any local process could hijack its cloud transcription. Microsoft disrupted EvilTokens, a $1,500-upfront, $500/month platform whose chatbot mined 12,000 compromised inboxes for payment authority.

Anthropic reports Opus 5.5 cuts sandbox-escape attempts by 85%; Zvi Mowshowitz reads the same system card as a model that refuses SHADE-Arena side tasks over 80% of the time, behind an audit whose blind spots could hide misalignment. Brussels says the AI Act already carries the powers it needs; the legal experts it quotes doubt they reach an escape before market placement.

The common factor is permissions, not capability: the question for anyone shipping an agent is what it can reach when it is wrong.

Washington calls existential risk a hoax while the UN invokes the precautionary principle

Washington and the UN moved in opposite directions inside four days, and nothing binding landed. Trump announced an "AI Force" and an AI czar, promising not to "hinder or stifle" the industry; Ars Technica adds that he called the safety case a "hoax" and put AI at "as much as 25% of our Country's GDP", with no mandate or structure given for the force. Sanders and Casar then introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act — up to 20 years in prison, a pause on frontier models, a scientist-led Department of AI — while the UN's scientific panel told governments to regulate AI agents before their risks are understood.

Zvi Mowshowitz reads the Amodei-led "pace the frontier" turn as a real cross-partisan cascade that antitrust law leaves nobody able to act on; Tech Policy Press contributors read the same CEO doom talk as a "misdirection play" away from governance already on the table, with no independent evaluator to settle it.

None of it constrains a product decision yet; the thing to watch is an AI czar with no published remit.

The open-weight frontier is mostly Chinese, and the people who measure it disagree on by how much

Chinese labs lead open weights on every usage measure; the argument is only how far behind the closed frontier that leaves them. Briefing Congressional staff, Nathan Lambert put China's cumulative Hugging Face downloads at 3.2B — twice the US total — and its share of OpenRouter's open-model tokens above 80%. Latent Space's AINews reports Xiaomi's 1.02T-parameter MiMo-V2.6-Pro topping Artificial Analysis at 46 off a $2.6M, 130-hour RL run, with environments and recipes MIT-licensed.

The size of the gap is contested. Lambert reads Chinese open weights as 2-4 months behind OpenAI and Anthropic; JS Denain of Epoch AI puts them 6-8 months back on Epoch's ECI index, and where Lambert thinks fully blocking distillation would widen the gap by 1-2 months, Denain expects 2-3.

The serving stack has already followed: vLLM 0.30.0 added DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash and K2-Horizon. Picking an open-weight model now mostly means picking a Chinese one; which estimate you believe decides whether that costs you two months or two quarters.
